## OctoAcme Project Management — Overview

OctoAcme follows a clear stage-based lifecycle that moves work from initiation through planning, execution, release, and retrospective. Initiation validates the business need and produces a Project One‑pager that identifies stakeholders, success metrics, and a high-level timeline. Planning breaks approved initiatives into a prioritized backlog, defines acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done, and maps release milestones and dependencies.

Execution emphasizes iterative delivery with small, focused work items tracked on a project board (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done). Developers create small PRs that link to issues and contain acceptance criteria; CI runs tests, linters, and security scans before review. Regular rituals — daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, demos, and sprint reviews — maintain visibility and enable timely escalation of blockers.

Roles and responsibilities are explicit to ensure clear ownership: Product Managers set outcomes and prioritize; Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, and stakeholder communication; Developers and QA deliver and validate work; Stakeholders provide approvals and strategic input. Risk management is maintained in a Risk Register and escalations follow a defined path (team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor). Quality is enforced through automated unit, integration, and smoke tests, manual QA when needed, and release checks that include rollback plans and post-deploy verification.
